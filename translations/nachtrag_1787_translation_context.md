## What this document is

The *Nachtrag von weitern Originalschriften...* (Munich, 1787, sold by Joseph
Lentner), the second of the three 1787 Bavarian government publications of
seized Illuminati papers. The title page (p.5) states its scope: papers
concerning the Illuminati sect in general, "ſonderbar aber" (particularly —
false-friend: not "strangely") its founder **Adam Weishaupt**, former professor
at Ingolstadt, discovered in the visitation of Baron Bassus's castle at
Sandersdorf ("einem bekannten Illuminaten-Neſte"), printed by the Elector's
express order and held in the secret archive for inspection on request. A
handwritten note by Baron Bassus on the packet itself (p.7) records that
Weishaupt had sent him these papers for safekeeping — the correspondence was
entrusted to a sympathetic nobleman and seized there. The volume is announced
as "Zwo Abtheilungen" (two parts); everything corrected so far belongs to Part
One ("Erſte Abtheilung. CORRESPONDENZ.").

Unlike the Anhang — one defense letter with a sympathetic editorial voice —
the Nachtrag is a prosecution-style compilation: it reprints the founder's own
correspondence in per-correspondent "case files," interleaved with hostile
editorial footnotes that name Weishaupt directly and accuse him of incest,
attempted abortion, corruption of youth, and intent to undermine religion and
the state. The letters date from January 1781 to early 1783, before the
order's 1784-85 suppression, mostly under the order's own Persian-style
calendar.

## People and correspondents

Guidance: translate/keep names as printed, don't anglicize (same rule as the
Anhang). Code names are proper names — render unchanged (Spartacus, Philo,
Celsus...). Keep variant spellings as printed (Hannibal/Annibal; Uliſſes /
Ulyſſes; "Sparcacus" on p.55, an apparent printer's typo). Redacted names stay
redacted — never guess them.

### Confirmed real identities
- **Adam Weishaupt ("Spartacus")** — the order's founder, "geweſener Profeſſor zu Ingolſtadt" (p.5; section heading "vom Spartacus (Weishaupt.)", p.9). Author of Section I's letters 1-24 (pp.9-106; Section II opens on p.107), the document's largest and most revealing run; writes from his code city "Epheſus" (strong inference that Ephesus = Ingolstadt: he calls himself "ein Profeſſor in Epheſus" marveling that he has become the teacher of Göttingen's professors, p.46 — flag, not asserted); claims authorship of the whole plan ("Der ganze Plan iſt von mir," p.88).
- **Adolph Freiherr von Knigge ("Philo")** — the Section II correspondent, per the epigraph ("Philo im Circulari an die Logen," p.6) and the section header "Briefe von der Hand- und Unterſchrift des Philo" (p.107); the real-name identification rests on general knowledge, not an in-corpus statement. Recruiter of ~500 members (pp.109, 111, 125), worked 16 hours daily for the Order (p.109), co-designer of the Priest/Regent grades, Spartacus's rival and reluctant reconciler.
- **Baron Baſſus** — owner of Sandersdorf; provenance note in his hand (p.7).
- **Joseph Lentner** — Munich publisher (p.5).
- **The Elector (Churfuͤrſt)** — unnamed; ordered the printing and archive placement (p.5); target of a proposed protectorate request for the "B== eclectiſchen ▢▢" (p.106).
- Real figures named plainly in passing (not code names; none yet cross-checked against secondary scholarship): **Nicolai** ("iſt nun auch beym ☉, et quidem contentiſſimus," p.36 — plausibly the Berlin publisher Friedrich Nicolai), **Boulanger** (author recommended to unsettle the Göttingen people, p.123), **Schloͤzer** (essay submitted for publication, p.135), **Wolter, Leveling** (named as victims of "unverzeihlichen Unbilligkeiten," p.132; Leveling plausibly the Ingolstadt professor — unverified), **Raynal** and **Machiavelli** (quoted in French, pp.59, 65-66), **Zinnendorf** (the rival Masonic rite's founder, pp.124, 143, 146), **Xenophon** (works lent, p.35), **Clandiopolis = Neuwied** (self-decoded, p.117).

### Redacted names (dash convention)
One or more em-dashes/equals signs stand in for names throughout — 30+ instances so far (p.15 "E⸗ ==="; p.19 "B. W=="; p.27 "V==="; p.28 "B.==", "A==", "D==", "G==", "S==="; p.34 "V===="; p.50 "F=="; p.60 "L===", "S=="; p.62 "W==", "B=", "S===", "D==="; p.69 "A--"; p.80-82 "A==", "F==", "G==="; p.96 "W---"; p.101 "W====", "G==", "F==", "D--", "W--"; p.106 "B==", "B---"; p.129-131 "P== E= von H==", "H== F=== von B==", "M====", "K=="; p.143-147 the numbered roster; p.150-151 "K— —" [a Canonicus], "St===" [a Kammerherr who swore at his cathedral chapter never to become a Mason], "K———" [a foreign brother]). Dash/equals counts vary per instance and are not meaningful; do not guess identities, and preserve the redaction in translation (e.g. "V==="). Variants: space-dash ("A--", "D--"), triple-dash category redaction ("mit ſchuldigen und unſchuldigen — — —", p.51), single letters ("L — —", p.46), and the "M.x.x." raised-dot notation (below).

## Register and tone guidance

Six distinct voices, and the translation must keep them separable:

1. **Spartacus's letters (Section I)** — intimate, urgent, self-dramatizing; swings between warmth, flattery, self-pity ("mich bis zur Verzweiflung treibt," p.22), grand claims ("in Zeit von einem Jahre weit herrlicher her," p.42), and hectoring; dense Latin tags and classical allusions; informal "ſie" address; instructional passages read like maxims ("Sitten-Regiment iſt von ganz eigener Art," p.12).
2. **Philo's letters (Section II)** — wounded pride, legalistic self-justification, cold enumeration of grievances and threats (the 13-point list, pp.120-122); letter 3, the "Kassel letter" (pp.126-132), switches to formal "Sie" and a lawyerly, near-clinical tone across five weeks and three datelines (Kassel, Braunschweig, Nentershauſen), including the "Sie"/"Ich" one-word paragraph headers as a rhetorical device (p.127 — preserve as formatting); letter 4 is reconciliation.
3. **Epictet's letters (Section III)** — deferential, measured, warmly social; the New Year letter (pp.139-142) mixes order business with domestic news; the only correspondent addressing Spartacus as "edler Goͤnner!"/"vortreflicher Freund!".
4. **Hannibal's letters (Section IV)** — energetic field reports addressed to the whole Areopagus ("Liebſte Areopagiten!"), businesslike enthusiasm, the numbered-cipher roster of recruits.
5. **R. Lullus's diary (Section VI)** — terse, telegraphic, formulaic dated minutes ("Den 10ten Meharmeh. Wurde allgemeine Minerval-Verſammlung... gehalten."); preserve the clipped style, don't smooth it into prose.
6. **The editorial footnotes (throughout)** — hostile, sarcastic, prosecutorial; third-person about Weishaupt; quote Latin Scripture (p.84) and Weishaupt's own published *Apologie* p.6 (pp.23-24); must read as visibly distinct from the letters — set off clearly in the formatting.

Register shifts within a letter (e.g. the informal-to-formal "ſie"/"Sie" distinction, Philo's Kassel letter) are signal, not noise; English loses the distinction, so annotate the shift rather than silently flattening it.

**Literal pass**: preserve sentence structure and word order where English allows, even if slightly stiff; keep ☉/▢ as symbols or bracketed glosses; keep Latin and French quotations in the original (they are part of the texture); don't resolve ambiguity the German leaves open; preserve page markers.
**Scholarly pass**: natural, readable English; render the symbols as "the Order" / "lodge(s)" with first-use glosses; translate Latin/French inline or in footnotes, retaining the originals in parentheses for the long block quotations (Machiavelli p.59, Raynal pp.65-66, the Latin pastiche p.84 — all flagged unverified against critical editions); keep the editorial footnotes distinct; keep the diary terse.
**Narrative pass (future)**: explain the code-name system, the two symbols, the Persian calendar, the case-file structure, and the publisher's hostile framing for a general reader.

## Faithfulness flags

Passages a translator must not render as settled fact (each is logged in the correction log):

- **pp.22-25**: the ciphered numeral sequences and their decodings ("meine Schwaͤgerinn iſt ſchwanger," "das Kind abzutreiben") — the digits were independently verified at 600 DPI and the decodings are the document's own footnotes, so they are reliable; but "Blutſchande" (incest) and the editorial characterization are the *publisher's* hostile framing, not established by the letter itself. Translate the letter's obliqueness as obliqueness; attribute the accusations to the footnote voice.
- **pp.23-24**: the block quotation from Weishaupt's *Apologie* p.6 is quoted by the hostile editor — keep it as a quotation within the footnote, not as Weishaupt's letter-voice.
- **p.7**: "Abſterbens" — a real gap in the printed page where "be" should be; reconstructed on lexical grounds.
- **p.11**: "Sache" vs. "Sachen" flagged as ambiguous in the pilot.
