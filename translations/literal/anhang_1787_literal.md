## Methodology & status
Translated by Claude (Sonnet 5) on 2026-08-08, directly from
`transcriptions/anhang_1787/anhang_1787_corrected.txt` (the Phase 3 corrected
German text). This is a **literal** pass: sentence structure and word order are
preserved close to the German wherever English allows, even where that reads
stiffly — the goal is traceability back to the source, not smooth prose (see
`translations/scholarly/` for the readable pass). Bracketed insertions `[...]`
mark words supplied for English grammar that aren't in the German, or a brief
clarifying note. This is an AI-assisted first pass, not a final scholarly
translation — pending human review, especially at points flagged uncertain
below. Page markers match the corrected German text for citation purposes.

## Translator's notes on a few recurring terms
- **"Extradition des Archivs"** (p.5-6): translated as "handover of the archive,"
  not "extradition" — context makes clear this is Zwack surrendering/transferring
  archive material himself (likely a formal handover of office papers), not the
  extradition of a person. Avoiding the modern false-friend reading.
- **Ordens-Progreſſen**: rendered "the Order's advances" throughout — a
  deliberately vague term Zwack himself is pushing back on; kept vague to match.
- **de Indiciis / Areopagiten / Locus Sigilli / etc.**: Latin terms kept as-is,
  per `translations/anhang_1787_translation_context.md`.

Court Councillor and Government Councillor at Landshut, upon the written

B.

Letter of the Baron von Dachsberg, Government President and Vicedom at
Landshut, to the Honorable von Zwackh.

Most Nobly Born, particularly Highly Esteemed Sir!

That Your Noble Lordship had the kindness toward me to promote my
dispatched petition regarding the travel-license, I render my most obliged
thanks; but the most gracious Resolution is still wanting to me, so I
likewise beg that you also be so good as to obtain it, and to bring it to
me upon your return; should you have to pay any fee for it, I shall
reimburse this at once.

I wish you all good things; your salary, which you surely deserve, then —

--- page 38 ---

you can administer the Lottery from here as well, and my modest powers
could contribute somewhat, so that it should never be lacking in
diligence on my part.

Should you, however, wish to go further from here, you would make me very
displeased; I do not at all mean to assert hereby that you should die as
Government Councillor — for once, this position would always be too small
for your knowledge, effort, and diligence. But consider your years,
and the age of your Chancellor, and you have a prospect that can scarcely
fail; I know very well what a Vicedom and a Chancellor ought to be — so if
I must speak before God and the world, then you are, in our present
situation, the first who can rightly demand such a post, without wishing
to deny that one or another may, in 10 to 12 years, grow up to it; but
Directorial positions ought to be filled only with already-formed persons,
etc.

--- page 39 ---

With which I entreat you for your constant grace and friendship.

Landshut, the 20th of July, 1786.

Your Noble Lordship's
obedient servant,
Dachsberg.
