# Nachtrag von weitern Originalschriften (1787) — Literal Translation

## Methodology & status
Translated by Devin on 2026-09-20, directly from
`transcriptions/nachtrag_1787/nachtrag_1787_corrected.txt` (the Phase 3 corrected
German text, PDF pages 5-152). This is a **literal** pass: sentence structure and
word order are preserved close to the German wherever English allows, even where
that reads stiffly — the goal is traceability back to the source, not smooth prose
(see `translations/scholarly/` for the eventual readable pass). Bracketed
insertions `[...]` mark words supplied for English grammar that aren't in the
German, or a brief clarifying note. Genuinely uncertain readings are flagged
inline with `[?]` rather than silently resolved. This is an AI-assisted first
pass, not a final scholarly translation — pending human review. Page markers
(`--- page N ---`, PDF page numbers) match the corrected German text and
`translations/nachtrag_1787_translation_context.md` for citation purposes. The
corrected text (and this translation) begins at PDF page 5 — pages 1-4 are not
part of the digitized text (title/cover matter not captured in the scan/OCR
pipeline; see the transcription's own provenance notes). Pages 5-152 were
translated on 2026-09-20. **Append 2026-10-02**: pages 153-340 translated by
Devin from the corrected German, continuing the same conventions (including the
two appendix documents of the "Zweyte Abtheilung" and the National-Directions
table on pp. 263; the corrected German itself breaks off mid-sentence at PDF
page 340 ("...und nicht im eigenen, ſondern"), and this translation stops at the
same point.

## Translator's notes on recurring terms
See `translations/nachtrag_1787_translation_context.md` for the full glossary
and register guidance; the following are the load-bearing conventions applied
throughout this pass:
- **☉** (circle-with-dot glyph): kept as the symbol, per the context file's
  finding that it renders "der Orden" (the Order) throughout. Not translated as
  "sun." Endings are kept attached as printed (e.g. "☉s" = "the Order's").
- **▢ / ▢▢**: kept as the symbol(s), rendering "Loge"/"Logen" (lodge/lodges).
- **Redacted names** (e.g. "V===", "B.==", "A=="): preserved exactly as printed,
  dash/equals-sign count included; never resolved or guessed.
- **Latin and French passages**: left in the original language, as in the
  source; these are direct quotations (Machiavelli, Raynal, Scripture pastiche,
  the letter headers' "S. d." formulas) that are part of the document's own
  texture, not translator's apparatus.
- **Code names** (Spartacus, Philo, Celsus, Cato, Marius, Diomedes, etc.):
  rendered unchanged as proper names, including inconsistent spelling variants
  (Hannibal/Annibal, Ulysses/Ulisses/Ulysses) exactly as the corrected text has
  them.
- **M. x. x.**: kept as printed (the raised-dot Masonic abbreviation
  hypothesis; see context file); not expanded.
- **☉-grade and Masonic-grade names** (Illuminatus minor/major/dirigens,
  Minerval, Regent, Priest, Scottish Knight, etc.): translated on first
  occurrence in a section and then given in the shorter German-derived English
  form used by the context file (Illuminatus minor, Priest grade, Regent grade,
  etc.), keeping abbreviations like "Ill. min." as printed where the letter
  itself abbreviates.
- **Persian-calendar month names and the "Jezdedgerd" era**: kept as printed
  (Benmeh, Dimeh, Meharmeh, Abenmeh, Merded, Thirmeh, Asphandar, Chardad,
  Pharavardin), not converted to Gregorian dates.
- **Code cities/provinces** (Ephesus = Ingolstadt inferred, Athens, Edessa,
  Erzerum, Thebes, Corinth, Samos, Utica, Olympia, Traonna; Pannonia, Illyria):
  kept as printed.
- **Editorial footnotes**: set off from the letter text exactly where the
  corrected German places them, translated with the same hostile,
  third-person, prosecutorial register as the original — attributed to the
  publisher's voice, not merged into the letter-writers' own words (see the
  context file's "Sensitive content" section).

I would, for the closer effecting of this, apply the following measures.

1) I would look about for steady, active, diligent men. I believe (24.) 3== [?]

to Celsus.

2) In the assemblies I would place at the head people who have standing,
authority, and know how to set the tone in [modes of] thinking, who are

3) I have had the recruiting carried out by people themselves capable for it,

6) I would distribute the young people among a steady, educated man for
instruction.

7) Those to whom they are assigned for instruction should never make

1.) That a certain union of all systems in the lower 3 degrees of the
Freemasons would come about, namely, that all lodges [▢▢], which worked

3.) That all sending of money to the higher superiors might cease.

4.) That a new law-book be made.

--- page 219 ---

5.) That the members of the lodges [▢▢] must elect their masters, and the
lodges [▢▢] their directoria.

6.) That no one, who claimed to have higher degrees, might on that account

silent about it, or hand over his wares.

others. But here was also at the same time laid the ground for the downfall
of freedom; equality disappeared. One felt new unknown needs; one also felt,
that they were not to be satisfied through one's own strength as before.

This is the short, true, and philosophical history of despotism and freedom,
