# Anhang — Translation Context (Phase 4, context-extraction pass)

Consolidates what Phase 3 correction already established, into guidance for the

## What this document is
A defense letter, dated Wetzlar, 20 May 1787, written by **Xaver von Zwack**
(confirmed by his own signature and an appended certificate, p.35-36) to a former
superior within the Illuminati order ("mein Freund," addressed throughout,
identity not stated). Zwack systematically rebuts specific items from the
government's *Einige Originalschriften* publication, point by point, defending
himself against accusations arising from the October 1786 search of his house at
Landshut. Two official documents are appended (Beylagen A and B): a certificate
of good conduct from three Bavarian court officials, and a routine 1786 letter
from Baron von Dachsberg. A second editorial voice — sympathetic to Zwack's
cause but independent enough to correct some of his specific claims — appears in
footnotes starting p.11 (see below).

## People (translate names as given, don't anglicize)
- **Zwack / Zwackh** — the letter's author. Both spellings appear in the source;
  keep as printed in each instance rather than silently standardizing.
- **Weishaupt** ("Spartacus") — the order's founder. Not directly named in the
  Anhang itself but relevant background.
- **Knigge** ("Philo") — not directly named in the Anhang; relevant background
  from the Nachtrag.
- **von Maßenhauſen** — Bavarian Court Chamber Councilor ("Hofcammerrath"),
  imprisoned by the time of writing; source of a scientific/technical materials
  collection discussed at length p.22-25.
- **Philipp Zwackhius** — Zwack's brother, a student.
- **Kanonikus Hertel** — a Canon, imprisoned alongside Maßenhauſen.
- **Pylades, Coriolan, Ajax** — order code names for individuals not otherwise
  identified; render as proper names, don't translate.
- **Graf Coſtanza** — a correspondent on order business.
- **Melchior Graf von Preysing, Carl Freyherr von Pauli, Carl Albrecht Edler von
  Vacchieri** — the three officials who sealed Zwack's certificate.
- **Freyherr von Dachsberg** — author of the appended Beylage B letter.
- Real external references Zwack invokes in his own defense: **Athanasius
  Kircher** (Jesuit polymath), the ***Theatrum Chemicum***, **Archenholz**
  (historian), **Goethe's *Sorrows of Young Werther***.

## Tone and register
Formal, personal, legalistic — a point-by-point rebuttal addressed to a trusted
former superior, mixing genuine emotional appeal (the suicide passage, the
farewell) with procedural/legal argument. Zwack alternates between defensive
irritation, wounded pride, and real tenderness (the closing farewell). The
sympathetic footnote-voice is drier and more argumentative.

**Literal pass**: preserve sentence structure and word order where English
allows, even if slightly stiff — prioritize traceability back to the German over
smoothness. Don't resolve ambiguity the German itself leaves open.

**Scholarly pass**: natural, readable English prose, unglossed 18th-century
diction updated where it would confuse a modern reader, but no content added,
softened, or summarized away. Footnote-voice passages should read as visibly
distinct from Zwack's own voice (e.g. set off clearly in the formatting).

## Sensitive content — handling instruction for both passes
Pages 22-25 (poison/abortifacient allegations and Zwack's rebuttal) and 28-31 (an
account of youthful suicidal ideation) must be translated with the same fidelity
and care as everything else — accurately and completely, not sanitized,
softened, or sensationalized. See the detailed annotations for context before
translating these sections.

`anhang_1787_corrected.txt`, so every sentence stays traceable to its source
